Connecting the CHSS Navigator to a district's Student Information System (SIS) gives the ninth grade team timely, accurate data through clear visualizations and a dashboard that updates automatically. The team can analyze, discuss, and intervene in minutes, without running reports, downloading files, or entering formulas. Few districts have a ninth grade dashboard of this kind, or the time and budget to build one. CHSS builds and maintains it for each partner school.

The cost of doing it by hand

Where this came from
  • James's collateral James's three-step routine and the 20-to-30-minute figure. His "no emails, no manual pulls, no file transfers" is reworded: SFTP is a file transfer, so the accurate claim is that nothing is manual once the automated feed runs.
  • Conversation James (10/23): "we waste 20 minutes in every coaching session running the Synergy report, exporting, opening, entering a bunch of Excel formulas." Only 20 of 178 partner schools had submitted first-quarter data as of 10/23.
  • James's collateral The Kid Talk figure is from James's 10/23 email: "Our own data shows us that when teams do a Kid Talk with fidelity they are 70%+ more likely to hit their on-track goal." Internal CHSS data; James and Tara to confirm the method and the permitted wording (Decisions, O16).
  • District lens A superintendent reading an earlier draft saw a credible value proposition and no evidence; one defensible result is what moves "acceptable" to "we want this."

Without an integration, nearly every coaching session begins the same way:

  1. The ninth grade lead runs a report from the SIS.
  2. The report is exported to a spreadsheet.
  3. The lead and the coach add formulas to identify which students are failing one or two courses.

That routine consumes 20 to 30 minutes of a 60-minute session. Half of the hour goes to clerical work rather than to students, and the data is often a full grading period old by the time anyone acts on it.

District staff carry a burden as well. Each quarter, CHSS asks a district data specialist to prepare a large export and upload it to a secure location. That takes time from the district's most technical people, and the data is months old by the time it is used. Once the automated feed is running, none of that work is manual: no emails, no one running reports for CHSS, and no one handling files by hand.

This matters because the protocol depends on it. In CHSS partner data, running the Kid Talk protocol with fidelity is associated with a more than 70 percent higher likelihood of meeting the school's on-track goal, and a Kid Talk is only as good as the data in front of the team. What SIS integration gives the school team

Where this came from
  • James's collateral The first five benefits are James's.
  • Conversation The last two were added from the 10/19 and 10/23 calls: Tara and Jonah on longitudinal tracking (on-track through CTE, dual credit, graduation) and on end-of-year data arriving in October today, which makes it "really hard to evaluate our staff, evaluate our impact, plan for the next school year."

Student data flows securely from the district's SIS into CHSS Navigator, where CHSS builds the ninth grade dashboards the team uses. Teams stop assembling data and start using it.

  • Time returned to students. The 20 to 30 minutes once spent preparing data goes back to the conversation about students.
  • Current data. Weekly updates once the feed is live: Friday's data for Tuesday's meeting, rather than last quarter's.
  • Accurate data. No manual exports or formulas means no transcription errors and no broken spreadsheets.
  • Faster action. Teams identify students failing one or two courses and plan support in the same meeting.
  • Visible progress. Teams can see whether each intervention is working and adjust before the next grading period.
  • A view beyond ninth grade. As the cohort advances, Navigator continues to follow it: on-track persistence in tenth grade, CTE course sequences, dual credit, and graduation. The district sees whether the ninth grade work held, and so does its board.
  • End-of-year results in June rather than October. Automated feeds mean on-track results are available when the next year is being planned, not after it has begun.

Most districts run reports by hand because no one has had time to establish the alternative. The integration work done together changes that:

  • $0 due to CHSS. The accelerator, the dashboard, and the integration work are fully subsidized for Year 0 and Year 1. The amount due from the district under the agreement is $0. What the work asks of district staff is stated plainly below.
  • CHSS sets it up with the district and stays until it works. CHSS technical staff meet with the district's data team, map the SIS exports to the Navigator format, and establish the feed. CHSS remains engaged until the feed passes acceptance, and returns when the district's SIS, grading scale, or term structure changes. Most districts need two to four working sessions over a few weeks; the district's data contact runs the export, and CHSS handles the mapping and the monitoring.
  • A path to owning district data. For a district that does not yet keep a nightly copy of its SIS data in a location it controls, the feed built together is a first step, and CHSS will share the routes other districts have taken. Once a district has that foundation, every future partner, dashboard, and analysis becomes easier.
  • Nothing to remember. Once the feed runs, no district employee runs a report for CHSS again. If a transfer fails, CHSS's monitoring detects it and CHSS follows up.
  • Clean records. The district receives an executed copy of every agreement, indexed, and a reminder before anything expires.

What CHSS asks of district staff. A technical contact and a privacy contact, named at acceptance. One onboarding call with both. The hours above to establish the feed, and more if the district needs the scheduled-upload route for a period. Nothing from teachers beyond the ninth grade team work the accelerator already requires. Data for the ninth grade cohort begins flowing in August 2027; the months before that are for putting the feed and the agreement in place.

  • District lens A California district technology and privacy lead (10/27 call) reads every partner agreement for four things "with no gray area": auto-renewals, what happens when data is compromised, destruction at the end of the contract, and selling or advertising to third parties. Each of the four is a bullet on this page.
  • Luke's decision See Decisions, M9, M18, and M19.

SIS integration is governed by a data sharing agreement with the district and designed to meet FERPA and the state's student privacy requirements. CHSS provides its own privacy and security appendix and will also sign the National Data Privacy Agreement (NDPA) in the state's version if the district prefers it. Student data remains the district's property, and the district decides who may see it.

  • Only the data needed. CHSS receives student ID, name, grade level and enrollment dates, course enrollments, course marks and credits by grading period, GPA, and attendance. Nothing else is required. Responses to the short belonging and skills screeners the school team administers, after the district approves the questions, are held under the same terms.
  • Used for one purpose. Identifiable student information supports ninth grade success work in the district's schools and nothing else. CHSS retains only de-identified and aggregate results to evaluate and improve the program.
  • Deleted when the district says so. When the partnership ends, or whenever the district asks, identifiable data is destroyed and the destruction is certified.
  • No automatic renewal. The data partnership ends with the agreement. Continuing it requires a new signature, not silence.

5.1 Purpose and relationship

Where this came from
  • FERPA School official exception, 34 C.F.R. § 99.31(a)(1)(i)(B): a contractor or other party to whom the institution has outsourced services may receive education records without consent if it performs a service the institution would otherwise use employees for, is under the institution's direct control as to use and maintenance, and is subject to the redisclosure limits of § 99.33(a).
  • Luke's experience On the 10/26 call: "there's a legitimate educational reason for you all to be looking at this data," and the coach already hears student names in every kid-talk today; the agreement "operationalizes" a threshold the district has already crossed.
  • AFUHSD precedent Luke's own test from the 360training review (Jan 2026): a partner is a FERPA school official when it is "an integrated part of learning" that the district facilitates, versus a direct student–vendor relationship like ACT or ASVAB.
  • AFUHSD precedent The sentence on parental consent is deliberate. AFUHSD's GCU priority-acceptance program (2025) had a board-approved agreement but no scalable consent mechanism and produced about one student in year one. Districts should not go looking for a consent process the school official exception doesn't require.
  • af-dpa knowledge/law/ferpa.md (captured 2026-10-26): PTAC adds a fourth element, that the party "has been determined to meet the criteria set forth in the school's or district's annual notification of FERPA rights for being a school official" (§ 99.7(a)(3)(iii)); many districts' annual notices name only employees and board contractors. The last sentence below asks the District to close that gap. The studies exception (§ 99.31(a)(6)) is the alternative basis for evaluation work, but it requires destruction when the study ends, which is why evaluation here rests on de-identified data instead.

The District and the Center for High School Success ("CHSS"), a program of Stand for Children Leadership Center, Inc. ("Organization"), a nonprofit organization exempt under section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, share student data under this Agreement so that CHSS can provide 9th Grade Success coaching, the CHSS Navigator platform, and on-track analytics to the District's participating schools (the "Services").

In providing the Services, CHSS and Organization act as a "school official" with a "legitimate educational interest" under the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA), 34 C.F.R. § 99.31(a)(1)(i)(B). CHSS performs an institutional service for which the District would otherwise use its own employees, is under the District's direct control with respect to the use and maintenance of education records, and will not redisclose student data except as this Agreement permits. The District's disclosure of education records to CHSS for the Services does not require parental consent under FERPA when the conditions in this Section are met; notice and consent requirements that apply to the screeners are governed by Section 6.7. The District will confirm that its annual notification of FERPA rights includes, among the criteria for school officials with a legitimate educational interest, contractors and organizations to which the District has outsourced institutional services.

5.3 Definitions

Where this came from
  • FERPA De-identified Data tracks 34 C.F.R. § 99.31(b)(1): records may be released without consent after removal of all personally identifiable information, provided the institution has made a reasonable determination that a student's identity is not personally identifiable, taking into account other reasonably available information.
  • James's MOU draft "Confidential Information" is retained from the current Section Five and narrowed to the defined terms below.
  • Conversation "Cohort" implements the 10/23 decision with James: start with ninth grade and "grow the dashboard each year after that" so the cohort can be followed into 10th grade, CTE sequences, and graduation.
  • Student Data means any information from the District's education records that identifies, or could be used to identify, a student, including the elements listed in Exhibit D-1. Student Data is Confidential Information.
  • Screening Data means responses and scores from the CHSS Belonging Screener and Skills Screener administered to students at participating schools. Screening Data is Student Data.
  • De-identified Data means Student Data or Screening Data from which all personally identifiable information has been removed in accordance with 34 C.F.R. § 99.31(b)(1), such that a reasonable person in the school community who does not have personal knowledge of the relevant circumstances could not identify the student.
  • Aggregate Data means statistics computed across groups of students, with any group smaller than ten students suppressed.
  • Navigator means the CHSS web platform through which the Services are delivered.
  • 9th Grade Success Team means the District employees at a participating school who are responsible for 9th grade students, as designated by the District.
  • Coach means the CHSS employee assigned to support a participating school.
  • Cohort means the 9th grade students enrolled at a participating school in a school year in which the Services are provided.

Part A. CHSS Data Security and Privacy Plan

Center for High School Success, a program of Stand for Children Leadership Center, Inc. Last updated [date]. This plan describes how CHSS protects the student data school districts share with it. Protocols and procedures may change as practices improve; the current version is always at [URL]. More detailed information about how CHSS handles a particular district's data is specified in the data sharing agreement signed with that district.

What CHSS receives and why

School districts share student records with CHSS so that 9th Grade Success Teams and CHSS coaches can identify students who are off track in 9th grade and act early. The data is limited to what the on-track work needs: enrollment, courses and marks, credits, attendance, and the elements a district elects for evaluation (Exhibit D-1 of the agreement). CHSS does not ask for Social Security numbers, government identifiers, payment information, or health records. Districts remain the owners of their data and decide who may see it.

Where the data lives

CHSS's infrastructure is entirely cloud-based, on industry-leading hosting and data-platform providers [such as Microsoft], with the Navigator application delivered on a commercial customer-relationship platform. Where possible, CHSS uses the providers' managed services rather than stand-alone servers, which lowers the attack surface and keeps patching with the provider. All student data is stored and processed in data centers in the United States.

How data gets to CHSS

Districts never send student data by email. Data moves by a scheduled transfer from the district's student information system or data warehouse to a CHSS SFTP server with key-based authentication, or by a read-only connection from the district's SIS vendor. Files are scanned and validated on receipt, and the district's technical contact receives a validation report after every load. The CHSS Technical and Data Integration Guide describes the files and the process.

Data security

CHSS maintains an information security program aligned with [CIS Controls v8.1, Implementation Group 1], a framework recognized by the National Data Privacy Agreement, and assesses itself against it annually. Measures include:

  • Encryption of student data in transit and at rest, including backups and the SFTP landing zone.
  • Multi-factor authentication for all CHSS accounts and for privileged access; role-based access with least privilege as the default.
  • Access to student data limited to the people who need it for the service: the district's 9th Grade Success Team sees its own school; the CHSS coach assigned to a school sees that school only; a small set of named CHSS technical staff administer the platform and investigate incidents; CHSS program, research, and leadership staff work only with de-identified data.
  • Logging of access to student data and of administrative actions, retained at least ninety days and available to a district for an incident investigation.
  • Security updates evaluated, validated, and applied according to severity, system risk, and operational impact, with the platform layer patched by the providers.
  • Endpoint protection on CHSS-managed devices; staff devices managed centrally.
  • A written incident response plan and a disaster recovery plan, tested [annually].
  • Daily backups held separately from the production environment, so data remains available in the event of ransomware; backups are restored only for continuity and recovery, and deletion obligations follow any restored data.
  • Security training for every employee at onboarding and annually; background checks for staff with access to student data, coaches included.

How CHSS ensures privacy

CHSS never sells student or district data and never uses it for advertising, profiling, or the advocacy, political, or fundraising work of any affiliated organization. CHSS does not share student data with program partners, evaluators, or funders unless the district has approved the recipient in writing.

CHSS restricts internal access to and use of district data. Only the CHSS staff who support a district's schools can see its student-level records, and every such access is logged. Student data is not used for development or testing.

When CHSS studies program outcomes, it uses only de-identified data, created by removing identifiers and replacing the student identifier with a random token under a key that is destroyed with the identifiable data. Before any de-identified data is kept or released, CHSS makes and documents the determination FERPA requires that a student cannot be identified from it, taking into account other reasonably available information.

Artificial intelligence. The Navigator platform uses approved AI and analytics capabilities to support educational analysis, next-step planning, and intervention recommendations for educators, who make all decisions about students. No student-identifiable data is used by CHSS or any provider to train any AI model, including any large language model. Incidents

If CHSS validates and confirms that student data has been accessed, acquired, disclosed, or lost without authorization, it notifies the district's privacy contact within seventy-two hours of that confirmation, handles the incident under its incident response plan, supports the district's notice to families, and bears the costs attributable to its own breach.

Retention and deletion

CHSS keeps a district's student data only for the term of the agreement. On the district's written directive, or within sixty days after the agreement ends, CHSS returns or destroys the district's identifiable student data from every system, including its providers, and certifies the destruction in writing. 1. Data integration overview

The CHSS integration has two phases, in this order:

  1. Rostering. Schools, students in scope, and the staff who will use Navigator. Three files, or a read-only connection to your SIS vendor where one is available.
  2. Student performance data for the on-track indicators and the 9th Grade Success Team's work: courses and marks, credits and GPA, attendance, summer school, state assessments, and the optional files the District elects in Exhibit D-3.

Both phases run through the same mechanism: scheduled CSV files placed on the CHSS SFTP server, or a scheduled export from your SIS vendor directly to CHSS (Exhibit D-2). CHSS processes files as they arrive. We ask that scheduled files land between [11 PM and 2 AM] District local time so that the morning's Navigator reflects the previous day.

Scope. All 9th grade students at participating schools, and, for each Cohort, the same students in later years until graduation or exit (Agreement Section 6.3). Send the full current roster each time; CHSS handles changes by comparing loads.

Cadence. Weekly during the school year, nightly where the District prefers; a final update within two weeks after the end of the school year and after summer school (Section 6.5). Per-file minimums are on each schema page.

What CHSS never asks for. Social Security numbers, government identification numbers, payment information, or health records (Appendix A.5). IEP, 504, and meal status only where the District elects them in Exhibit D-3.

4. CSV file conventions

These conventions cover CHSS's own file formats. If your SIS produces a standard export that does not follow them, tell your integration contact; CHSS can map a vendor-standard export rather than ask you to transform it.

File format.

  • Send every file as CSV (comma-separated values) with the .csv extension, UTF-8 encoded, with a header row.
  • Surround every field with double quotes. This is the single most common source of rejected rows (names with commas, course titles with commas).
  • Column headers must match the schema exactly: lower case, words separated by underscores (student_sis_id), no spaces.
  • Dates as YYYY-MM-DD. School years as the four-digit ending year (2026-27 is 2027).
  • Leave a column blank rather than omitting it. Headers are always present.
  • Be careful saving CSVs from Excel. Excel changes long identifiers to scientific notation and strips leading zeros. Export from the SIS or warehouse directly.

File naming. File names are fixed per schema and tell CHSS how to process the file. They are listed on each schema page. Do not add dates or district codes to the file name; CHSS records the receipt time.

File location. Files placed in your /automated directory are processed within [5 to 10 minutes] of arrival. Files placed in /test are validated and reported on but not loaded, for checking an export before scheduling it.

Full or incremental. Each schema page says whether a file is replacing (the file is the complete, current source of truth; records absent from the new file are archived) or additive (the file adds or updates records and never removes them). Rostering files are replacing. Score and event files are additive.

Code tables. Four small files sent once and again when they change: grading scale, terms and grading periods, attendance codes, and (if elected) discipline codes. Schemas in Section 8.11.

The rule

A commitment stays in the contract only if one of three tests is met:

  1. The NDPA already requires it. Then it is not restated, and the supplement must not contradict it.

Everything else is technical documentation: dated, provided on request under the evidence clause, and not a contractual promise. Documentation is still read by districts, so it has to be accurate on the day it is given.

Jonah asked whether A.12(a) prevents CHSS from using the data to make Navigator smarter. Wording now in v1.7 (position 2). "(a) neither CHSS nor any Subprocessor will use Student Data, or any output derived from Student Data, to train, fine-tune, or otherwise improve any artificial-intelligence or machine-learning model, and neither will develop synthetic or inferred data from Student Data for any purpose other than delivering the Services to the District; this paragraph does not limit CHSS's use of De-identified Data and Aggregate Data, created and reviewed under A.10, for research and development of the Services and to demonstrate their effectiveness, including improving the on-track indicators, screeners, and intervention suggestions the Services provide, provided that this work stays within CHSS and no model provider or other Subprocessor receives or retains De-identified Data for it." The line is drawn at the model, not at the Services: nothing identifiable trains any model anywhere, and what CHSS learns from de-identified data stays inside CHSS.

Why each phrase is there, and two consequences for Tara
  • "Neither CHSS nor any Subprocessor": the model provider is bound through CHSS, since a district cannot contract with the provider.
  • "Student Data, De-identified Data, or any output derived from them": closes the de-identify-then-train route. California's NDPA version 1.5 (Exhibit G, clause 8) bars training and the development of synthetic or inferred data; Colorado's terms bar training on Student Data outright.
  • "train, fine-tune, evaluate, or otherwise improve": evaluation sets are how models are tuned without "training" in name; the list prevents relabeling.
  • "for any purpose other than delivering the Services": attaches to synthetic and inferred data, not to training. Clause (b) then permits the student-specific inferences Navigator produces, treated as Student Data.
  • What the clause does not prevent: evaluating the program with de-identified and aggregate data (5.5, A.10); learning from results and encoding what works as rules, content, or configuration.
  • No separate dataset floor: A.10 already requires the documented 34 C.F.R. § 99.31(b)(1) determination before any De-identified Data is retained, and suppresses cells under ten. The 1EdTech template's twenty-individual floor is an alternative if a district asks for a number.
  • For Tara, two consequences. Most of the eighteen state terms, Ohio and Colorado among them, limit use to the services described in the NDPA's Exhibit A, so "evaluation and improvement of the Services using de-identified data" and the outcome measures she names must appear there. Iowa's terms say the NDPA "does not provide approval for use of Student Data for research or audit/evaluation purposes" and require the district's institutional review board approval first.

This plan describes how the Center for High School Success (CHSS) protects the personally identifiable information it receives from school districts and other local education agencies (each, an "LEA") to provide ninth grade success coaching and the CHSS Navigator platform. 1. Purposes for which CHSS uses the data

Where this came from
  • State law 8 NYCRR § 121.3(c)(1) requires "the exclusive purposes for which the student data or teacher or principal data will be used." The TEC agreement's New York terms limit use to the services in Exhibit A.
  • Drafting choice Mirrors the draft Exhibit A on the Notes tab and Appendix A.4. The list is exhaustive on purpose: in six TEC states nothing outside it is permitted.

CHSS uses the data it receives from an LEA only for the following purposes, and for no other:

Administrative.

  • Access to student data is limited by role to the people who need it to provide the services: the LEA's designated school staff; the CHSS coaching staff assigned to that school, for screening and intervention work only; and a small, named set of CHSS technical staff who administer the platform and investigate incidents. Technical.

  • Multi-factor authentication is required for every CHSS account; LEA users may sign in through their LEA's single sign-on.
  • Every access to student-level records and every administrative action is logged with user, time, and record; logs are protected from alteration, retained for at least twelve months, and available to the LEA on request.
  • Production systems are segregated from the internet by firewall and network controls, deny traffic by default, and receive critical security patches within seven days of release.
  • Anti-malware protection is current on every endpoint with access to student data.
  • Stored passwords are salted and hashed and are never visible to CHSS staff.
  • Changes to the platform go through code review and testing, and credentials are kept in a secrets vault, never in code.

Operational.

  • LEAs send data by encrypted SFTP or HTTPS secure upload; email is never used to transfer student data.
  • Every incoming file is virus-scanned and validated before it is loaded.
  • Backups are encrypted, tested, and expire on a documented rotation of no more than ninety days; they are never restored to production except to recover from an incident.
  • Software-generated intervention suggestions are recommendations for educators, who make all decisions about students. Student data, de-identified data, and outputs derived from them are not used to train, fine-tune, or improve any machine-learning model, by CHSS or any subprocessor. 7. Managing and reporting incidents

Where this came from
  • State law § 121.6(a)(6): the plan must specify "how the third-party contractor will manage data security and privacy incidents that implicate personally identifiable information including specifying any plans to identify breaches and unauthorized disclosures, and to promptly notify the educational agency." § 121.10 sets an outer limit of seven calendar days after discovery and requires the contractor to pay or reimburse the cost of notification for a breach attributable to it.
  • SDPC NDPA Article V.4: notice within seventy-two hours of confirmation, a written incident response plan, and the LEA notifies families; the New York terms add the number of records affected, a description of the investigation, a point of contact, and provider-paid notification costs.
  • Drafting choice Seventy-two hours satisfies the NDPA, New York, Ohio, and California. The parent-notification procedure is Appendix A.8, added to meet California Education Code § 49073.1(b)(6).

Detection. CHSS monitors access logs, administrative activity, file-transfer activity, and security alerts from its subprocessors, and investigates anomalies. Staff are trained to report any suspected incident to the Security Officer immediately.

Response. CHSS maintains a written incident response plan and tests it at least annually. For each incident CHSS assesses scope and severity, contains it, preserves evidence, recovers affected systems, documents what happened, and reviews and updates its practices.

Notice to the LEA. CHSS notifies the LEA's designated contact by telephone and email without unreasonable delay and no later than seventy-two hours after confirming any unauthorized access to, or acquisition, disclosure, or loss of, the LEA's data, whether or not it meets a statutory definition of breach, unless law enforcement requires a delay. The notice includes, as known: the nature and date of the incident, the types of data and number of records involved, the students and staff affected, the steps taken to contain it, the investigation so far, and a point of contact. CHSS updates the LEA as the investigation proceeds and cooperates with the LEA, state privacy officers, and law enforcement.

Notice to families. The LEA decides whether and how affected students, parents, and staff are notified. CHSS supplies the list of affected individuals and a draft notice, sends the notice in the LEA's name if the LEA directs it, and pays the cost of notification and related remedies for any incident attributable to CHSS or its subprocessors.

8. Return, transition, deletion, and destruction

Where this came from

Duration. CHSS keeps an LEA's identifiable data only for the term of its agreement with that LEA. The data provisions do not renew automatically.

At the LEA's direction. At any time, on the LEA's written directive (NDPA Exhibit D, or Exhibit D-4 of CHSS's agreement), CHSS returns the specified data in a commonly used format such as CSV, transfers it to a successor the LEA names, or destroys it, within sixty days.

At termination or expiration. Copies in encrypted backups expire on the documented rotation of no more than ninety days. Destruction follows NIST SP 800-88 guidelines.

M21. Appendix A carries California's nine required terms, mapped

Where this came from
  • State law California Education Code § 49073.1(b), enacted by AB 1584, requires nine terms in any district contract for digital storage, management, or retrieval of pupil records or for educational software that lets a third party access pupil records: (1) district ownership and control; (2) pupil control of pupil-generated content, if applicable; (3) no use beyond the contract's purposes; (4) parent review and correction procedures; (5) security actions including designation and training of responsible individuals; (6) procedures for notifying affected parents of unauthorized disclosure; (7) certification of non-retention at completion and how it is enforced; (8) how the district and third party jointly ensure FERPA compliance; (9) no targeted advertising.
  • District lens A California colleague shared the CETPA/Fagen Friedman & Fulfrost AB 1584 compliance checklist (10/27), which is how California district counsel review a partner agreement: nine boxes. A San Diego reviewer will tick them in order.
  • Luke's decision Six of the nine were already in Appendix A. Three were implicit and are now explicit: a described parent-notification procedure after a breach (A.8), named responsible individuals with a line in Exhibit D-3 (A.7), and a joint FERPA compliance paragraph (A.2). The pupil-generated-content term is answered "none held" (A.5) rather than left to inference. The California bullet in A.17 maps all nine to their sections so the review takes five minutes instead of an afternoon.
